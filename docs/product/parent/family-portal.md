# Family Portal (Parent)

The Family Portal is a **warm window** onto one child, built for a parent's phone. It is not the classroom PC and not the School Office. Parents cannot award stars, buy artifacts, or see the teacher’s private Chronicle.

**Pro and Elite.** One login per student, created by the teacher under Teacher Settings → **Family Access** or by the School Office under Admin → **Family Logins**.

**Getting in.** Families scan the school's **Parent sign-in QR** (on the poster or their login slip). It opens the sign-in screen, **The Quest Gate** (which shows the school’s name on a banner under the keystone; a school with its own code remembers it on that phone, and its sign-in links and QR codes carry the code), straight on the **Parent** door with a "Hello, family!" welcome; they type the username and password from the slip. That phone remembers the Parent door next time. If a detail is wrong, the screen says so in plain words (for example, "That username and password don't match") and **Forgot password?** tells them to ask the teacher or the office for a new one.

If the School Office records that the child has left the school, the login is paused (the records are kept). If the child comes back, the login works again — unless the teacher had already switched it off.

Phones and computers share one layout: a header with the child’s portrait, first name, class, guild and hero class, and a tab bar at the bottom. The header sky follows the time of day. **Check for news** asks the school for a fresh summary; the portal also does this by itself each time a family opens it.

## The four tabs

| Tab | What families see |
|-----|-------------------|
| **Home** | A postcard that sums up the week in plain words (stars this week, a test coming up, the latest result, the next lesson), then what is **new since the last visit** on this phone. Below: the **next lesson** (from the class days and time, skipping school holidays) and the next **test day** or homework; the **star jar** for this month; the latest **published** note from the teacher with **Reply**; and **star moments**, the recent reasons stars were given |
| **Homework** | The current Quest Assignment pinned like a note on the fridge, titled by the lesson it is for. A test in the assignment gets its own card with a countdown, the topics, and **Add to calendar** (a reminder the evening before) |
| **Progress** | Stars month by month as marks on a height chart; a **report card** with each test or dictation, its score and an average ring (only if this class records assessments); attendance with the dates missed; and the child’s hero class, guild, level and gold, with **How the class game works** for families |
| **Messages** | **Write to the school** with a topic: **A question**, **Ask to meet**, **Absence note** (it starts the sentence with the next lesson’s date), or **Just a note**. Conversations look like a chat; unread ones are marked on the tab bar |

If the class does **not** use tests or dictations, Progress says so and tells the story through stars and attendance.

A conversation a family starts reaches the child’s teacher and the **School Office** (Messages in the office). The office can reply there.

## What parents should understand (so teachers can say it)

- Stars are **character in English class**, not a second official grade.
- Monthly stars reset; the year’s stars and the guild do not.
- Homework here is **the next lesson’s quest**, not a government worksheet dump.
- Messages are for meetings, questions and absences, not for arguing a Prodigy result in the last week of the month.

## Dignity

The portal is **parent-safe by design**. Do not publish notes you would not read aloud at a desk with the child present. Star moments and kept promises belong here; Skill Tree tactics do not. Corrections you make, such as repairing a score, never appear as star moments.
